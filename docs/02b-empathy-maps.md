# 02b — Empathy maps: Edinburgh heating customers

The main finding is that the research has almost no customer voice from period or tenement flat owners, and none at all from owners of listed buildings. Map 1 rests on a single forum thread from 2019, with 18 posts about electric-heated tenement flats. Map 2 rests on about 80 reviews, but none of those reviews say what kind of property the customer lives in. So the split between the two maps is not clean: some Map 2 reviewers probably live in tenements too.

**Evidence base.** One source: the CityCyclingEdinburgh Forum thread "Central Heating in flats" (March 2019, 18 posts). Everything below comes from that thread unless marked otherwise.
- It covers **electric-heated tenement flats** only. Nothing covers *listed* buildings or period features as a customer concern.
- The prices quoted in it are out of date.
- Forum users skew toward one demographic (a cycling forum), which may inflate the carbon theme.

## Comparison: does the period-flat group deserve its own content?

**Is it distinct? Yes, but only on one axis.**

**OBSERVED.** The two groups talk about different things:
- Map 1 posters talk about **the property and the heating system**: fuel type, whether there is a gas supply, running cost and install cost.
- Map 2 reviewers talk about **the tradesperson**: whether he turns up, whether he's honest, whether he's tidy.
- Map 1 has no evidence at all about how these owners choose an engineer.

**INTERPRETED.**
- The difference probably comes from the stage each group is at, not from who they are. Map 1 posters are deciding *what to do*. Map 2 reviewers have already decided and are judging *who did it*.
- A tenement owner almost certainly ends up caring about no-shows and mess too, but this research can't show that.
- Building constraints (flues, stairs, listed status) don't appear in customer voice at all. They appear only in official guidance and in competitors' copy.

**Is it big enough? Unknown.** Nothing in this research measures how many Edinburgh customers live in tenement, period or listed flats. Sizing it needs official housing or listing data, which is a separate research task.

**Recommendation: one page, not three.**
- The evidence supports a single page called **"Replacing electric or storage heating in an Edinburgh flat"**. It should answer the forum questions and include a short, carefully sourced section on flues in listed buildings and conservation areas.
- Don't build separate listed-building or tenement pages yet. No customer has asked those questions in the evidence we have.
- Add more pages once his own jobs and enquiries show demand. He should log the property type on every enquiry from day one, because that's the cheapest way to answer the sizing question.

## Questions period-flat owners actually ask (evidenced)

All of these come from the CityCyclingEdinburgh thread (2019):
1. Should I avoid buying a flat with electric heating, or can I put central heating in?
2. Is there a gas supply to the flat, and what happens if there isn't?
3. Roughly what does central heating cost in a 1-bed tenement flat?
4. Will my bills be higher on electric than on gas?
5. Are modern storage heaters any better than old ones?
6. Is gas the right choice at all, given carbon and other fuel options?
7. What if the flat still has lead pipes? This is implied by the lead-pipe-removal job one poster described.

**Likely questions with no customer evidence.** These are INTERPRETED: they come from official guidance and competitor positioning, not from customers. Treat them as hypotheses to test against his own enquiries:
- Where can the flue go on a listed building, and do I need consent?
- Can a boiler or flue go on the front of a building in a conservation area?
- How does a new boiler get up a tenement stair?
- Where does the condensate pipe go?

## Check with official sources before publishing

**1. Listed building consent for flues.**
- The Council's October 2022 guidance says balanced flues are "not normally acceptable" on front or conspicuous elevations, and that consent is required there. It sits on a 2023 consultation site, so **check whether it has since been revised or replaced.**
- The September 2025 householder guidance says flues on listed buildings "will likely require" consent. That wording is vague. **Ask City of Edinburgh Council planning whether rear flues on listed buildings need consent, and in which cases.**

**2. Conservation areas where the building is not listed.**
- **The research found nothing on this.** Every flue rule quoted above is about listed buildings.
- Check the permitted development rules for flues, external gas pipes and condensate pipes on flats in conservation areas. Flats may have fewer permitted development rights than houses.

**3. External pipework.**
- No source covers gas service pipes, condensate pipes or other pipe runs on elevations. The only clue is a 2014 Gazette notice, where a consent application covered a gas service pipe plus four rear flues.
- That suggests pipework can be part of the consent question, but it doesn't confirm a rule. **Check before saying anything about pipework.**

**4. Internal works in listed flats.**
- Not researched: for example, pipe runs through original features, or moving the boiler position.
- Check with the Council or Historic Environment Scotland whether these trigger consent.

**5. Shared walls and common property in tenements.**
- Not researched: for example, whether owners need neighbours' agreement for a flue through a common external wall.
- Check the title deeds position and the Tenements (Scotland) Act 2004 before giving advice.

**6. Technical flue siting.**
- Not researched: distances from windows and boundaries, plume, and when a building warrant is needed.
- Check the Gas Safe guidance, manufacturers' instructions and the Scottish Building Standards technical handbook.

**7. New gas supply.**
- The only customer evidence is a 2019 forum post saying it's "rather long-winded".
- Get the current process and timescales from the gas network operator before publishing. *(Step 3 adds SGN's free supply check and a standard new-connection estimate of £3,157 + VAT; see `03-search-landscape`.)*

**8. Funding facts.**
- Check these with Home Energy Scotland on the day of publishing:
  - the 30 November 2022 gas exclusion;
  - the heat pump grant and loan amounts;
  - the £8,000 high heat retention storage heater condition;
  - whether Warmer Homes Scotland still includes gas boilers.
- Show a "last checked" date on the page.

**9. Prices.**
- Don't publish the forum figures ("4500 in 2013", "£55/mo", "thousands"). They are years out of date.
