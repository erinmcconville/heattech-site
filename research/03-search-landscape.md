## 4. Scottish funding, law and Edinburgh flue facts

### Home Energy Scotland funding (checked 27 October 2026; RE-CHECK before publishing)

OBSERVED
- Home Energy Scotland Grant and Loan is for eligible owner-occupiers of their only or main home, not ordinary buy-to-let landlords.
- Up to £7,500 grant plus optional up to £7,500 interest-free loan for qualifying heat pumps or heat-network connections.
- Eligible insulation measures can attract up to 75% grant funding, subject to caps and criteria.
- Ordinary gas boiler installation is not a qualifying measure under the standard grant and loan. Some other schemes may support central heating, including gas, for specifically eligible households.
- Private Rented Sector Landlord Loan: separate from the homeowner scheme.
- Warmer Homes Scotland: can help eligible low-income households with measures including central heating.

Sources:
- https://www.homeenergyscotland.org/home-energy-scotland-grant-loan
- https://www.homeenergyscotland.org/find-funding
- https://www.mygov.scot/energy-saving-funding/warmer-homes-scotland-scheme

### Scottish gas boiler rules

OBSERVED
- **Existing homes:** on 18 November 2025 the Scottish Government said introduction of its proposed Heat in Buildings Bill was paused, and it intended to revisit legislation after the May 2026 election. **RE-CHECK: that election has now taken place; the position may have changed.** Older proposals (e.g. a 2030 date) are not current law.
- **New-build properties:** the New Build Heat Standard prohibits fossil-fuel gas and oil boilers as the main heating system in new buildings within its scope, while allowing some secondary heating. This doesn't apply to like-for-like replacement in an existing home.

Sources:
- https://www.gov.scot/publications/heat-in-buildings-plans/
- https://www.gov.scot/publications/new-build-heat-standard-factsheet/

### Listed buildings, conservation areas and flues in Edinburgh

OBSERVED
- A flue through an external wall is an external alteration. For listed buildings, even small external works may require listed building consent, and planning permission may also be needed. City of Edinburgh Council tells homeowners to check listed status and conservation-area designation and review its guidance before starting work.
- Whether a specific non-listed Edinburgh flat needs planning permission depends on the building, proposal, elevation, designations and permitted-development provisions. England's Planning Portal flue rules don't apply in Scotland.
- For tenements, flue terminal location, distances/ventilation, roof/wall access and shared-fabric ownership are also relevant.

Sources:
- https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/conservation-2/listed-buildings
- https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/listedbuildingconsent
- https://consultationhub.edinburgh.gov.uk/sfc/conservation-adaptation2023/supporting_documents/Listed_Buildings_Guidance_14_October_2022.pdf

### Gas supply

OBSERVED
- SGN offers a free check of whether a gas service pipe exists and can be used. A new connection may need further work and a supplier-installed meter.
- SGN's standard Scottish new-connection estimate: £3,157 + VAT if SGN digs trenches (eligibility and physical assumptions apply). Second-floor-or-higher flats can need non-standard quotes. Not a price for any specific tenement; excludes boiler, radiators and pipework.

Sources:
- https://www.sgn.co.uk/gas-connections/household-customers/find-out-if-you-have-gas-supply
- https://www.sgn.co.uk/gas-connections/household-customers/apply-new-gas-connection0
