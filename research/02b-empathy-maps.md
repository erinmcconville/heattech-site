# 02b — Empathy maps: Edinburgh heating customers

Customer evidence grouped by property type.

CAVEAT: there is almost no customer voice from period or tenement flat owners, and none from owners of listed buildings. Map 1 rests on one forum thread (2019). Map 2 rests on about 80 reviews that don't state property type, so some Map 2 reviewers probably live in tenements too.

## Map 1: Period, listed or tenement flat owners

**Evidence base:** CityCyclingEdinburgh Forum, "Central Heating in flats" (March 2019, 18 posts). All entries come from this thread.
CAVEATS: covers electric-heated tenement flats only; nothing on listed buildings or period features; prices OUT OF DATE; one forum's demographic (cyclists) may inflate the carbon theme.

| Quadrant | OBSERVED |
|---|---|
| SAY | "the bills will be a lot higher than gas" · old storage heaters "were like burning fivers for heat" · central heating in a 1-bed tenement flat costs "thousands, but not tens of thousands" |

## Differences between the two maps

OBSERVED
- Map 1 posters discuss the property and heating system: fuel type, gas supply, running cost, install cost.
- Map 2 reviewers discuss the tradesperson: turning up, honesty, tidiness.
- Map 1 has no evidence about how these owners choose an engineer.
- Building constraints (flues, stairs, listed status) don't appear in customer voice. They appear only in official guidance and competitors' copy.

CAVEATS
- The difference may reflect stage (deciding what to do vs judging who did it) rather than customer type. Not testable with this evidence.
- The size of the period/tenement/listed segment in Edinburgh is not measured.

## Questions customers asked (evidenced)

All from the CityCyclingEdinburgh thread (2019):
1. Should I avoid buying a flat with electric heating, or can I put central heating in?
2. Is there a gas supply to the flat, and what happens if there isn't?
3. Roughly what does central heating cost in a 1-bed tenement flat?
4. Will my bills be higher on electric than on gas?
5. Are modern storage heaters any better than old ones?
6. Is gas the right choice at all, given carbon and other fuel options?
7. What if the flat still has lead pipes? (Implied by one poster's job.)

ASSUMPTION — questions with no customer evidence (from official guidance and competitor copy only):
- Where can the flue go on a listed building, and is consent needed?
- Can a boiler or flue go on the front of a building in a conservation area?
- How does a new boiler get up a tenement stair?
- Where does the condensate pipe go?

## Unverified regulatory areas

Not confirmed by the research. Anything on these topics is unverified until checked with the named source.

1. **Listed building consent for flues.** CEC Oct 2022 guidance says balanced flues are "not normally acceptable" on front or conspicuous elevations and need consent there. It sits on a 2023 consultation site; may have been revised. Sept 2025 householder guidance says flues on listed buildings "will likely require" consent. Whether rear flues on listed buildings need consent is unconfirmed (source to check: CEC planning).
2. **Conservation areas, non-listed buildings.** Nothing found. Permitted development rules for flues, external gas pipes and condensate pipes on flats are unchecked.
3. **External pipework.** No source. One 2014 Gazette notice covered a gas service pipe plus four rear flues in one consent application.
4. **Internal works in listed flats** (pipe runs through original features, moving the boiler). Not researched (CEC / Historic Environment Scotland).
5. **Shared walls and common property in tenements.** Not researched (title deeds; Tenements (Scotland) Act 2004).
6. **Technical flue siting** (distances, plume, building warrant). Not researched (Gas Safe guidance, manufacturers' instructions, Scottish Building Standards technical handbook).
7. **New gas supply.** Customer evidence is one 2019 post ("rather long-winded"). SGN facts are in `03-search-landscape.md`.
8. **Funding facts.** RE-CHECK with Home Energy Scotland on the day of publishing: the 30 Nov 2022 gas exclusion; heat pump grant and loan amounts; the £8,000 high heat retention storage heater condition; whether Warmer Homes Scotland still includes gas boilers.
9. **Prices.** Forum figures ("4500 in 2013", "£55/mo", "thousands") are OUT OF DATE.
